# Electronic structure and relativistic PV

## Structure and parity-conserving screen

1. Record atom mapping, isotope masses, charge, protonation, coordination, explicit solvent, counterions and source for every geometry. Generate B by reflecting the whole A model, including any explicit environment. Retain the atom correspondence and verify inversion of all stereogenic elements. Build different diastereomers as independent models. Sample SAP/TSAP, ring and pendant-arm motion, hydrated and unhydrated states and plausible ligand-loss products. For charged species test sensitivity to solvent and counterion models.
2. Optimize representative structures with a documented scalar-relativistic treatment. Use frequencies to check minima where feasible and assess relative free energies and exchange barriers with uncertainty. Cross-check against structure and speciation data when available. Use precisely reflected structures for parity checks because independently optimized structures can introduce numerical asymmetries. With the PV operator off, compare total and transition energies and test rotation, integration grid and convergence sensitivity.
3. For Yb(III), start with a state-averaged CAS(13,7) over the seven 4f orbitals, containing seven spin-free doublet roots in the isolated 4f manifold. Treat spin–orbit coupling and inspect the resulting four ground and three excited Kramers doublets associated with the ²F₇/₂ and ²F₅/₂ multiplets. Compare ORCA CASSCF/SOC or OpenMolcas RASSCF/RASSI with resolved spectra. Expand to relevant ligand and metal orbitals when covalency or charge transfer matters, assess dynamic correlation, and track states by overlaps and character. The minimal space is a model for multiplet assignment. Quantitative core properties need separate validation. [S1, S2](references.md#software)
4. For Re(I) and Ir(III), use a relativistic ground-state model and initial excited-state screen. Identify metal-to-ligand, ligand-to-ligand, intraligand and metal-centred contributions via transition densities or natural transition orbitals. Benchmark candidate roots with SOC and a multireference treatment as needed, and test the sensitivity to functional, solvation and active space. Distinguish vertical excitation, relaxed emission and the measured zero-phonon or vibronic line. [T1–T3](references.md#transition-metal-comparators)

## Weak-interaction calculation

Use a finite-nucleus relativistic Hamiltonian and evaluate the nuclear-spin-independent operator proportional to $G_F\sum_A Q_W(A)\rho_A\gamma^5/(2\sqrt{2})$, summed over electrons and nuclei. Record the operator's overall sign convention, constants, weak charges by isotope, normalized nuclear density and nuclear radii, gamma-matrix convention, and whether any spin-dependent term is present. A spin-zero isotope is a useful starting point for disentangling hyperfine structure. An ordinary SOC calculation alone does not include this operator. [PV1, PV2](references.md#parity-violation-and-edm)

- Evaluate state-specific matrix elements with a four-component all-electron approach or a validated two-component treatment with the transformed operator. Confirm the chosen DIRAC release or implementation supports the *energy operator* and the required open-shell/excited-state treatment. Other parity-odd properties in a manual do not establish that support. An effective-core-potential screen requires separately validated near-nuclear reconstruction before being used for PV. [S3](references.md#software)
- Start with the run details of a published small-molecule benchmark. Demonstrate agreement in units, sign and size under matching conventions. Cross-check a direct expectation value against a symmetric finite-field slope where supported, converging field amplitude and root tracking. Test inversion sign reversal, invariance to proper rotations and a symmetry-enforced achiral null. Flag residuals before examining target sizes. Near-degenerate states may require state interaction. Keep Kramers and isotope/hyperfine conventions explicit.
- Report $W_g$ and $W_e$ for both enantiomers before computing the difference of transition energies in Hz. Propagate shared uncertainty and cancellation. Converge basis including tight functions on heavy atoms, correlated core shells and virtual cutoffs, SOC, nuclear model, geometry, solvation and vibrational/conformer averaging. Record whether the sign survives these changes. If a suitable excited-state implementation cannot be validated, record the calculation as blocked rather than extrapolating from Yb atomic data or optical activity.
